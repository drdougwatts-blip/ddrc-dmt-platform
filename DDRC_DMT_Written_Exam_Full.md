## Section 1: Multiple Choice Questions (40 marks)

*Choose the single best answer for each question. Each question is worth 1 mark.*

### Diving Medicine and Decompression Illness

**1.** Shortly after surfacing from a 40m bounce dive, a diver reports rapidly worsening pins and needles in both legs and a band-like pain around his waist. Which of the following best describes his condition?

A) Static neurological DCI with girdle pain
B) Acute cutaneous DCI
C) Progressive neurological DCI with girdle pain
D) Relapsing lymphatic DCI

**2.** After an uncontrolled ascent to the surface, a diver using nitrox rapidly becomes confused and semi-conscious. What is the most likely diagnosis?

A) Acute static limb pain DCI
B) Spontaneously improving neurological DCI
C) Arterial gas embolism
D) Progressive cutaneous DCI

**3.** During a closed bell dive, the bellman complains of a headache and is sweating. His breathing rate is 24 per minute. What is the most likely cause?

A) Hyperoxia
B) Hypercapnia
C) Carbon monoxide poisoning
D) Hyperthermia

**4.** A saturation diver at 90 msw storage depth complains of worsening knee pain that started 6 hours ago. The pain is constant but not getting worse. There are no neurological symptoms. How would you classify this presentation to the shore-based physician?

A) Static musculoskeletal DCI
B) Progressive neurological DCI
C) Acute pulmonary barotrauma
D) Compression arthralgia

**5.** You are treating a diver in the recompression chamber. The recommended maximum oxygen partial pressure for treatment is:

A) 1.4 bar absolute
B) 1.8 bar absolute
C) 2.4 bar absolute
D) 2.8 bar absolute

**6.** Subcutaneous gas formation around the neck after a dive is most consistent with:

A) Mediastinal emphysema
B) Subcutaneous emphysema
C) Tension pneumothorax
D) Interstitial emphysema

**7.** Which of the following is an early indication that a diver may be experiencing CO2 retention?

A) Thoracic pain
B) Elevated breathing rate
C) Reduced level of consciousness
D) Headache

**8.** The most commonly reported form of barotrauma in divers is:

A) Pulmonary
B) Aural (middle ear)
C) Dental
D) Gastric

**9.** You are administering IV fluids to a patient in a recompression chamber. Which of the following is NOT a practical concern?

A) Low chamber ceiling limiting gravitational head for the drip
B) Ensuring all air is removed from IV lines before infusion
C) Maintaining adequate fluid levels in the drip chamber
D) Increased resistance to fluid flow due to ambient chamber pressure

**10.** In a recompression chamber, the cuff of a supraglottic airway or endotracheal tube should be filled with:

A) Air
B) Petroleum jelly
C) Saline
D) Water-based lubricant

**11.** A diver complains of aural discomfort during chamber pressurisation. Your immediate action should be:

A) Continue pressurisation at a slower rate
B) Stop pressurisation and consult the physician immediately
C) Vent the chamber to surface pressure
D) Stop pressurisation and vent back in 0.5m increments until comfortable

**12.** The main operational concern with using helium in breathing mixes is:

A) Increased narcotic potential
B) Increased heat transfer from the diver
C) Decreased gas solubility in tissues
D) Vocal distortion affecting communication

**41. [5 marks]**

You are the DMT on a dive vessel at a remote offshore location. Ten minutes after surfacing from a 30 metre dive, one of the team complains of feeling sick and slightly dizzy with a worsening band-like pain around his lower back. The supervisor confirms that all required decompression was completed. There is a fully functional recompression chamber and medical kit on site.

List five initial actions you would take to manage this casualty.

**42. [2 marks]**

Referring to the patient above, how would you describe his condition to the shore-based physician using current DCI terminology?

**43. [3 marks]**

You are the DMT in the recompression chamber during treatment of the diver above. During decompression from 18 msw to 9 msw, the patient reports chest tightness and difficulty taking a full breath.

Describe the three actions you would take immediately, in order of priority.

**44. [4 marks]**

You are first responder to an explosion in a machinery space aboard a DSV. After confirming it is safe to enter, you find four casualties:

Casualty 1: 37-year-old male, superficial burns to right leg and chest, very distressed, calling for help.
Casualty 2: 20-year-old male, pinned by a steel beam across his chest, unresponsive, occasional weak gasps, deeply cyanosed.
Casualty 3: 18-year-old male, obvious deformity of the lower left leg with active bleeding, conscious, pale.
Casualty 4: 45-year-old female, minor cuts and abrasions, sitting against a bulkhead, pale with audible stridor.

Using triage principles, list the order in which you would prioritise treatment (first to last) and briefly state why for each.

**45. [4 marks]**

A 40-year-old deckhand has been struck in the right side of the chest by a crane load that swung in a swell. He has paradoxical chest wall movement on the right side, is taking only shallow breaths, and is in obvious pain. His vital signs are: BP 140/80, HR 100, RR 30. Breath sounds are diminished on both sides. He is pale.

(a) What is your diagnosis? (1 mark)
(b) What causes the paradoxical movement? (1 mark)
(c) What are your immediate management priorities? (2 marks)

**46. [4 marks]**

You are working in saturation when a colleague tells you he is not feeling well. Using a systematic approach, list four observations you would make and/or questions you would ask in your initial assessment.

**47. [3 marks]**

List three clinical situations in which a DMT might need to insert a urinary catheter in a patient.

**48. [4 marks]**

While working on a vessel in temperate waters (8-12 degrees C) at night, a crew member falls overboard. He is wearing a life jacket and foul weather clothing. He is recovered from the water after a 30-minute search.

List four priorities for his rescue and immediate management.
