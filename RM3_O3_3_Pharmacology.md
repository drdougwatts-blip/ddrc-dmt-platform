Analgesics

Simple analgesics
Strong analgesics
Paracetamol: oral, 1g 4-6 hourly (max 4g/24h)
Ibuprofen: oral, 400mg 8 hourly with food
Combination: paracetamol + ibuprofen for moderate pain
Morphine: IM 10mg or IV titrated (physician guidance required)
Respiratory depression risk: monitor SpO2 and respiratory rate
Controlled drug: documentation and two-person check
Entonox: covered separately (specific D020 requirement)

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
Cardiac, Resuscitation, and Emergency Drugs
Adrenaline: anaphylaxis (IM 0.5mg), cardiac arrest (IV 1mg per cycle)
Atropine: symptomatic bradycardia
Aspirin: suspected acute coronary syndrome (300mg chewed)
GTN spray: chest pain with physician guidance
All emergency drug doses should be memorised or immediately accessible
In cardiac arrest: follow the ALS algorithm under physician direction

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
Antibiotics and Other Drugs

Antibiotics
Other drugs
Oral antibiotics: for wound infection, UTI, respiratory infection
Choice depends on suspected organism and site
Always with physician guidance: do not self-prescribe
Allergy check before administration (especially penicillins)
Antiemetics: ondansetron, cyclizine (seasickness, post-operative)
Antihistamines: chlorphenamine (allergic reactions, not anaphylaxis)
Dexamethasone: cerebral oedema, severe allergic reactions
Salbutamol: bronchospasm (inhaler or nebuliser)
Diazepam: status epilepticus (physician direction only)

IV Fluids and Entonox

IV fluids
Entonox (50% N2O / 50% O2)
Normal saline (0.9%): standard resuscitation fluid
Hartmann's solution: alternative crystalloid
Indications: hypovolaemic shock, burns, dehydration
Rate and volume: physician guidance
Practical IV cannulation is taught in-person
Self-administered analgesic gas
Rapid onset, rapid offset
Contraindicated in pneumothorax (N2O expands gas spaces)
Contraindicated in head injury (raised ICP risk)
Contraindicated in DCI (N2O worsens bubble expansion)
D020 specifically requires Entonox coverage

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
Drug Safety and Documentation

Safe handling
Documentation
Check expiry dates: expired drugs must be replaced
Storage: temperature-sensitive items, light-sensitive drugs
Controlled drugs: locked storage, register, two-person check
Sharps safety: safe disposal of needles and ampoules
Record every drug administered: drug, dose, route, time, who gave it
Record who authorised it (physician name and contact time)
Record the patient's response to the drug
Drug errors: report immediately, contact physician, document
Do not try to cover up a mistake. Honesty protects the patient.

Key Takeaways
Know the DMAC 15 drug list: indications, routes, doses, contraindications

Entonox: effective but contraindicated in pneumothorax, head injury, and DCI
Drug safety: check expiry, document everything, report errors honestly
