## Scenario Brief: DCI in Saturation

**Setting:** DSV operating in the North Sea. Saturation system at 90 msw storage depth. The patient is a 38-year-old saturation diver, 4 hours into an RN62 treatment table at 18 msw. Dive control reports the diver is complaining of leg weakness.

**Patient Presentation:** Conscious, alert (GCS 15) but anxious. Reports bilateral leg weakness developing over the past 20 minutes. Unable to stand. Tingling in both feet. No headache, no visual disturbance. Previous history: one episode of Type I DCI 3 years ago, treated and resolved.

**Vital Signs (provided when measured):** HR 92, BP 138/82, RR 18, SpO2 97% on chamber air, Temp 36.8, BGL 5.4

**Neurological Findings (when examined):** Upper limbs normal. Lower limbs: reduced power bilaterally (3/5 hip flexion, 4/5 knee extension), reduced sensation to light touch below L2 dermatome, brisk knee reflexes, plantars equivocal.

**Physician Instructions (SI role-plays when called):** Request full neuro exam results. Request vital signs. Instruct change to 100% O2 via BIBS. Instruct candidate to prepare IV access (normal saline, keep-vein-open rate). Request hourly neuro reassessment. Consider extending the treatment table. Confirm DMT is documenting on DMAC 01.

---

## Scenario Administration

Candidates are assessed in pairs. Each pair has approximately 25 minutes (including setup and changeover). For a class of 8, expect 4 pairs taking approximately 2 hours total.

The SI role-plays the physician on the phone. The SI should be consistent across all pairs: provide the same information when asked, give the same instructions, and respond to questions with the same level of detail. Do not volunteer information the candidate has not asked for.

The patient role can be played by a candidate from a previous pair, a volunteer, or (for the neurological findings) described verbally by the SI. The neuro exam should be performed on a live person where possible, with the SI providing the abnormal findings when asked.

## Pass / Fail Determination

**Pass requires BOTH of the following:**

1. No score of 0 (not demonstrated) in any area marked CRITICAL (areas 1, 2, 6, and 7).
2. An average score of 2.0 or above across all 10 competency areas.

**Automatic fail:** A score of 0 in any critical area is an automatic fail regardless of the overall average.

**Borderline candidates (average 1.8 to 2.0):** Discuss with the course director before communicating results. Options include a brief viva voce on the specific weak areas, or deferral to the next course. Document the decision and rationale.

## Scenario Variations

Use different variations for subsequent pairs or subsequent courses. The marking criteria remain the same regardless of which variation is used.

**Variation A: Upper Limb and Cranial Nerve Involvement**
Patient presents with facial droop (CN VII) and unilateral hand weakness in addition to or instead of lower limb findings. Same management principles. Tests ability to perform a more comprehensive neuro exam including cranial nerves.

**Variation B: Presentation During Decompression**
Symptoms develop during routine decompression from saturation, not during a treatment table. Adds the decision point about whether to halt decompression. Physician will instruct to stop decompression and hold depth. Tests understanding of treatment vs decompression contexts.

**Variation C: Deterioration During Assessment**
Patient initially presents as per the standard scenario but deteriorates during the assessment: develops respiratory distress (increasing RR, dropping SpO2) suggesting possible pulmonary involvement. Tests ability to escalate, reassess, and manage an evolving situation under pressure.

The examiner should use the same scoring sheet and pass/fail criteria for all variations. The marking guide assesses competency areas, not scenario-specific actions. A candidate who performs well on any variation has demonstrated the required competencies.
