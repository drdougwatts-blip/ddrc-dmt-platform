### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
01
Primary Survey: ABCDE
Find and fix life threats in order

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
A: Airway
Is the airway open? Is the patient talking?
Causes of obstruction: tongue (most common), blood, vomit, foreign body, swelling
Interventions: head tilt chin lift, jaw thrust, suction
Adjuncts: OPA, NPA (practical in-person Day 1)
Advanced: i-gel, BVM (practical in-person Day 1)
Fix airway problems before moving to B

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
B: Breathing
Look: chest movement, symmetry, rate, depth, effort
Listen: air entry both sides, added sounds
Feel: tracheal position, subcutaneous emphysema
Life threats: tension pneumothorax, flail chest, open chest wound
Count the respiratory rate. Normal: 12-20 per minute
Oxygen: give it early if there is any breathing concern

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
C: Circulation
Feel a pulse: present? Rate? Rhythm? Strength?
Skin: colour, temperature, capillary refill time (normal < 2 seconds)
Bleeding: is there major external haemorrhage?
Control catastrophic bleeding: direct pressure, tourniquet, haemostatic agents
Signs of shock: tachycardia, hypotension, pallor, confusion, cold peripheries
IV access and fluids: under physician guidance

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
D: Disability
AVPU: Alert, responds to Voice, responds to Pain, Unresponsive
Pupils: size, equality, reactivity to light
Blood glucose: check in any patient with altered consciousness
Lateralising signs: is one side weaker than the other?
GCS: more detailed scoring (covered in later module)
Think: DCI, stroke, head injury, hypoglycaemia, drug effects

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
E: Exposure
Fully examine the patient: front and back, head to toe
Remove clothing as needed (maintain dignity)
Temperature: use a thermometer (hypothermia is easily missed)
Log roll if spinal injury suspected: examine the back
Look for injuries you might otherwise miss
Then cover the patient: prevent heat loss

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->

Fix each life threat before moving to the next letter.
Do not jump ahead. Do not skip steps.
A patient with an obstructed airway will die before you get to check their blood pressure.

Secondary Survey
Head-to-toe examination and SAMPLE history

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
Secondary Survey

Head-to-toe examination
SAMPLE history
Only after all life threats are managed
Systematic: scalp, face, neck, chest, abdomen, pelvis, limbs, back
Look, feel, move (where appropriate)
Document everything you find
Takes 5-10 minutes if done properly
S: Signs and symptoms
A: Allergies
M: Medications
P: Past medical history
L: Last oral intake
E: Events leading to injury or illness

Examining a Diver
What makes a diving casualty different

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
Diver-Specific Assessment
Neurological examination: critical for DCI detection (rapid and full versions)
Diving history: depth, time, gas mix, decompression profile, symptoms onset
Previous diving incidents: any history of DCI?
Baseline observations: pulse, RR, BP, SpO2, temperature, blood glucose, urinalysis
Examination in confined spaces: what is possible in a bell or chamber?
Repeat assessments: detect deterioration or improvement over time

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
Vital Signs: What to Measure and Record

Core observations
Additional observations
Pulse: rate, rhythm, strength
Blood pressure: systolic and diastolic
Respiratory rate: count for 60 seconds
SpO2: pulse oximetry (limitations in CO poisoning)
Temperature: low-reading thermometer for hypothermia
Blood glucose: BM stick (any altered consciousness)
Urinalysis: dipstick testing
Pupil size and reactivity
Skin colour and temperature
Level of consciousness: GCS or AVPU

04
On-Site Management
Principles of casualty care before transfer

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
Management Principles
Safety: scene safety, PPE. Do not become a second casualty.
Positioning: recovery position, sitting upright for breathing difficulty, legs elevated for shock
Oxygen: give it when in doubt. High flow, reservoir mask.
Warmth: prevent further heat loss. Blankets, shelter, warm environment.
Reassurance: talk to the conscious patient. Explain what you are doing.
Spinal precautions: immobilise if mechanism suggests spinal injury
When not to move: unless the scene is unsafe or access requires it

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 18 -->
Patient Movement in Diving Operations

Within the dive system
External transfer
Bell to chamber: through small hatches, limited space
Chamber to vessel medical facility: may require decompression first
Patient packaging under pressure: what equipment fits?
Stretchers and boards: know what is available on your system
Helicopter: altitude restrictions for DCI, noise, vibration, limited space
The DMT may or may not escort the patient
Handover to receiving medical team: use DMAC 01 structure
Coordinate with supervisor for vessel operations

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
Choking and Airway Obstruction
Partial obstruction: patient can cough, speak, breathe (encourage coughing)
Complete obstruction: silent, cannot cough or speak, clutching throat
Conscious patient: up to 5 back blows, then up to 5 abdominal thrusts, alternate
Unconscious patient: start CPR (chest compressions may dislodge the obstruction)
Practical technique will be drilled in-person during the BLS session on Day 1

Key Takeaways
ABCDE is non-negotiable: use it for every patient, every time, in order
Fix life threats before moving to the next letter
Secondary survey only after the primary survey is clear
SAMPLE gives you the history structure when your brain is under pressure
Diver-specific assessment: neuro exam and diving history are essential additions
