# DDRC Pocket Reference Cards

*Supplementary Material - For offshore use after course completion*

---

## Card 1: DMAC 01 Aide-Memoire Quick Reference

| Section | Key Information to Record |
|---|---|
| **1. Patient Details** | Name, age, role, company, vessel/rig, date and time of incident |
| **2. History of Incident** | What happened, when, depth, duration, gas mix, decompression completed? |
| **3. Symptoms** | Time of onset, nature, location, progression, severity (0-10) |
| **4. Vital Signs** | HR, BP, RR, SpO2, temp, GCS/AVPU, BGL, pupils |
| **5. Neuro Exam** | Rapid neuro: orientation, speech, facial symmetry, power (all 4 limbs), sensation, coordination, gait |
| **6. Treatment Given** | O2 flow rate, IV fluids (type, volume, rate), drugs given (name, dose, route, time) |
| **7. Dive Details** | Profile, table used, stops completed, previous dives in 24h, flying/altitude exposure |
| **8. Medical History** | SAMPLE: Symptoms, Allergies, Medications, Past history, Last oral intake, Events |
| **9. Physician Contact** | Who contacted, time, advice given, follow-up plan |

**Record times throughout. Document negative findings. Write legibly. Keep original with patient.**

*Full DMAC 01 form available from dmac-diving.org. This card is an aide-memoire, not a replacement.*

## Card 2: Rapid Neurological Examination

Perform in this order, top to bottom. Takes 5-10 minutes. Record all findings on DMAC 01.

| # | Test | Method | Normal Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Orientation | Ask: name, where they are, date/time | Correct answers to all three |
| 2 | Speech | Listen during conversation | Clear, fluent, making sense |
| 3 | Facial symmetry | Ask to smile, raise eyebrows | Both sides equal |
| 4 | Eye movements | Follow finger: up, down, left, right | Full range, no double vision |
| 5 | Upper limb power | Grip your fingers, push and pull arms | Equal strength both sides |
| 6 | Lower limb power | Push feet down and pull up against resistance | Equal strength both sides |
| 7 | Sensation (hands) | Light touch on both hands | Feels equal both sides |
| 8 | Sensation (feet) | Light touch on both feet | Feels equal both sides |
| 9 | Coordination | Finger to nose (eyes closed), heel to shin | Smooth and accurate |
| 10 | Gait | Walk 5 steps, turn, walk back | Steady, no deviation |

**Any abnormality = contact physician immediately.** Repeat at regular intervals and record changes. Compare left vs right throughout.

## Card 3: ABCDE Primary Survey with Diving-Specific Additions

| Step | Standard Actions | Diving-Specific Considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **D - Danger** | Scene safety. Power off. PPE. Number of casualties. | Gas supply status? Chamber pressurised? Hazardous atmosphere? Rescue team available? |
| **R - Response** | Shake and shout. AVPU scale. | If in water: recover to surface/bell. If in chamber: assess through viewport or via comms. |
| **A - Airway** | Head tilt/chin lift (jaw thrust if trauma). Suction. OPA/NPA. i-gel if needed. | Remove helmet/mask. If in chamber at pressure: standard airway kit works. Vomiting under pressure: risk of aspiration. Suction early. |
| **B - Breathing** | Look, listen, feel. Rate, depth, effort. SpO2. Auscultate. O2 15L NRB mask. | Pneumothorax after rapid ascent? Tension pneumothorax signs? Pulmonary barotrauma? O2 toxicity seizure (remove O2, protect, will self-resolve). |
| **C - Circulation** | Pulse (rate, rhythm). BP. Capillary refill. Control bleeding. IV access. Fluids. | DCI: horizontal position, do NOT elevate legs. IV fluids (NS or Hartmann's). Hypothermia: handle gently, warm slowly. Immersion pulmonary oedema. |
| **D - Disability** | GCS/AVPU. Pupils. BGL. Rapid neuro exam. | Neuro exam critical for DCI detection. Compare sides. Document carefully. Repeat and record progression. |
| **E - Exposure** | Undress to examine. Temperature. Head-to-toe check. Prevent heat loss. | Hypothermia likely in water casualties. Wet suit removal. Active warming. Skin: suit squeeze? Marine stings? Check ears (barotrauma). |

**After ABCDE:** Contact physician. Give O2 100%. Start DMAC 01. Monitor. Prepare for evacuation or recompression.

## Card 4: DMAC 15 Drug Dose Quick Reference

*Administer under physician guidance only*

### Analgesics

| Drug | Route | Adult Dose | Frequency | Max / 24h | Key Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paracetamol | Oral | 1g | 4-6 hourly | 4g | First-line. Safe in diving. |
| Ibuprofen | Oral | 400mg | 6-8 hourly | 1.2g | Avoid if renal risk, GI bleed. |
| Diclofenac | IM / Oral | 75mg IM | 12 hourly | 150mg | IM for acute pain. GI/renal caution. |
| Morphine | IM / SC / IV | 10mg IM | 4 hourly | As directed | Resp depression risk. Titrate IV slowly. |

### Emergency

| Drug | Route | Adult Dose | Frequency | Max / 24h | Key Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Adrenaline** | IM | 0.5mg (0.5ml of 1:1000) | 5 min repeat | As needed | Anaphylaxis. Anterolateral thigh. |
| Aspirin | Oral | 300mg | Single dose | 300mg | Chest pain / suspected MI only. |
| GTN spray | Sublingual | 1-2 sprays | 5 min repeat | 3 doses | Chest pain. Sit patient down first. |
| Salbutamol | Inhaled | 2-10 puffs | As needed | As directed | Asthma / bronchospasm. Via spacer. |

### Anti-emetics / Anti-inflammatory / Other

| Drug | Route | Adult Dose | Frequency | Max / 24h | Key Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cyclizine | IM / Oral | 50mg | 8 hourly | 150mg | First-line anti-emetic. |
| Ondansetron | IM / IV / Oral | 4-8mg | 8 hourly | 24mg | If cyclizine ineffective. |
| Dexamethasone | IV / IM | 8mg | As directed | As directed | Cerebral oedema. Physician decision. |
| Chlorphenamine | IM / Oral | 10mg IM | As directed | 40mg | Allergic reaction (non-anaphylaxis). |

### Antibiotics

| Drug | Route | Adult Dose | Frequency | Max / 24h | Key Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Co-amoxiclav | Oral | 625mg | 8 hourly | As directed | Broad spectrum. First-line. |
| Flucloxacillin | Oral | 500mg | 6 hourly | As directed | Skin / soft tissue infections. |
| Ciprofloxacin | Oral | 500mg | 12 hourly | As directed | UTI / GI infections. Physician choice. |
| Chloramphenicol | Topical eye | 1 drop | 2 hourly | As directed | Eye infections only. |

**All drugs administered under physician guidance. Check allergies first. Record drug, dose, route, time on DMAC 01. Check DMAC 15 for current list.**

## Card 5: Teed Classification - Middle Ear Barotrauma Grading Scale

Grade middle ear barotrauma by otoscopic appearance of the tympanic membrane (TM).

| Grade | Otoscopic Appearance | Symptoms | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Normal TM. No abnormality visible. | None or mild fullness | Nil |
| **1** | Erythema (redness) along the handle of the malleus only. | Mild discomfort, ear fullness | Monitor |
| **2** | Erythema of the entire TM. Slight haemorrhage within the membrane. | Pain, reduced hearing | Rest from diving. Review. |
| **3** | Gross haemorrhage within the TM. Membrane swollen. Dark red or purple. | Significant pain, hearing loss, possible tinnitus | **No diving. Physician review.** |
| **4** | Free blood in the middle ear (haemotympanum). Dark TM. Blood may be visible behind membrane. | Severe pain, hearing loss, vertigo possible | **No diving. Urgent physician review.** |
| **5** | Perforation of the TM. Hole visible. Blood or fluid discharge from the ear canal. | Sudden pain relief then hearing loss, possible vertigo, nausea | **No diving. ENT referral.** |

**Always record grade, ear (L/R/bilateral), and time of examination.**

Inner ear barotrauma (sensorineural hearing loss, persistent vertigo, tinnitus) is a separate and more serious condition requiring urgent specialist referral regardless of TM appearance.

*Return-to-dive decisions are always made by the physician, not the DMT.*

## Card 6: Normal Vital Signs - Adult Reference Ranges

| Parameter | Normal Range | Concern If | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Heart Rate** | 60 - 100 bpm | < 50 or > 120 bpm | Palpate radial pulse for 30 seconds, multiply by 2. Note rhythm (regular/irregular). |
| **Blood Pressure (systolic)** | 100 - 140 mmHg | < 90 or > 160 mmHg | Use correct cuff size. Patient seated, arm supported. Record systolic/diastolic. |
| **Respiratory Rate** | 12 - 20 /min | < 10 or > 25 /min | Count for 30 seconds without patient awareness. Note depth and effort. |
| **Oxygen Saturation (SpO2)** | 94 - 100% | < 94% | Pulse oximeter on finger. Unreliable if cold, poor perfusion, nail polish, CO exposure. |
| **Temperature** | 36.0 - 37.5 C | < 35.0 or > 38.5 C | Use low-reading thermometer for diving casualties (standard thermometers miss hypothermia). |
| **Blood Glucose** | 4.0 - 7.0 mmol/L (fasting) | < 3.5 or > 11.0 mmol/L | Capillary finger-prick. Non-fasting up to ~11.0 may be normal postprandial. |
| **Pupils** | Equal, round, reactive to light (PEARL) | Unequal, fixed, or sluggish | Check in dim light. Compare sides. Document size in mm if possible. |
| **GCS / AVPU** | GCS 15 / Alert | GCS < 15 or V/P/U | GCS: Eye (4) + Verbal (5) + Motor (6) = 15. AVPU for rapid screening. |
| **Capillary Refill Time** | < 2 seconds | > 2 seconds | Press fingertip for 5 seconds, release, count. Prolonged = poor perfusion. |

**Measure and record a full set of vitals on first contact and at regular intervals thereafter. Trends matter more than single readings.**
