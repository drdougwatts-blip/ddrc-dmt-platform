## Section 2: Short Answer Marking Guide

### Q41. [5 marks] - DCI Initial Management

Award 1 mark for each of the following (any 5):
- DR ABCDE primary survey / systematic assessment
- Administer 100% O2 via demand valve or non-rebreather mask
- Place the patient supine (recovery position if reduced consciousness)
- Establish IV access and begin fluid resuscitation (normal saline)
- Perform a rapid neurological examination and document findings
- Contact the shore-based physician / diving medical specialist
- Complete DMAC 01 aide-memoire for communication with medical services
- Prepare for recompression (do not delay for other interventions)
- Continue monitoring vital signs at regular intervals

Do NOT award marks for vague answers such as "keep them comfortable" or "reassure the patient" without specific clinical actions.

### Q42. [2 marks] - DCI Classification

Award 2 marks for: Progressive (or acute progressive) neurological DCI with girdle pain.

Award 1 mark for answers that correctly identify DCI with neurological involvement but use outdated terminology (e.g. "Type 2 bend").

Award 0 marks for answers that fail to identify the neurological component or the progressive nature.

### Q43. [3 marks] - Chamber Treatment Complication

Award 1 mark per action, must be in a logical order:

1. Inform the chamber operator immediately to halt decompression and hold depth (or recompress to the previous depth)
2. Assess the patient: airway, breathing, circulation; look for signs of deterioration (tension pneumothorax, pulmonary O2 toxicity, worsening DCI)
3. Contact the supervising physician with a DMAC 01 update including vital signs and symptom description

Accept reasonable variations in order provided the first action addresses stopping the decompression. Do NOT accept "wait and see if symptoms improve" as a first action.

### Q44. [4 marks] - Triage

Award 1 mark per correct placement with rationale:

1. **Casualty 4** - Airway compromise (stridor indicates partial upper airway obstruction; immediate life threat that is treatable)
2. **Casualty 3** - Circulation (active bleeding with signs of shock; life-threatening but controllable haemorrhage)
3. **Casualty 1** - Delayed (superficial burns, distressed but no immediate life threat; stable)
4. **Casualty 2** - Expectant (mechanism suggests unsurvivable injury; agonal breathing with cyanosis in a patient pinned by structural debris; resources directed to salvageable patients first)

Accept reasonable rationale variations. The key is that Casualty 4 is treated before Casualty 3 (airway before circulation), and Casualty 2 is lowest priority.

### Q45. [4 marks] - Chest Trauma

**(a)** Flail chest (1 mark). Accept "multiple rib fractures with flail segment".

**(b)** A segment of the chest wall has lost bony continuity with the rest of the ribcage (typically 2 or more ribs fractured in 2 or more places). The free segment moves in the opposite direction to the rest of the chest during breathing. (1 mark)

**(c)** Award 1 mark per action (any 2):
- High-flow oxygen (100% via non-rebreather)
- Stabilise the flail segment (positioning the patient on the injured side, or manual/padding stabilisation)
- Establish IV access and begin fluid resuscitation if shock develops
- Monitor for tension pneumothorax on the affected side
- Arrange urgent evacuation / contact physician
- Pain management as directed by physician (Entonox if not contraindicated, or analgesia per DMAC 15)

### Q46. [4 marks] - Saturation Colleague Unwell

Award 1 mark per relevant observation/question (any 4). Expect answers from the SAMPLE/ABCDE framework applied to a saturation context:

- Signs and symptoms: What exactly are you feeling? Where? When did it start? Getting better or worse?
- Allergies: Any known allergies?
- Medications: Are you taking anything? (including self-medication)
- Past medical history: Any previous medical problems?
- Last oral intake: When did you last eat or drink?
- Events: What were you doing when this started?
- Vital signs: measure pulse, BP, temperature, respiratory rate, SpO2, blood glucose
- Visual assessment: colour, sweating, posture, respiratory effort

Do NOT require SAMPLE to be named explicitly. The candidate should demonstrate a structured approach, not just list random questions.

### Q47. [3 marks] - Catheterisation Indications

Award 1 mark per correct situation (any 3):
- Neurological DCI affecting bladder function (urinary retention)
- Spinal cord injury causing neurogenic bladder
- Unconscious patient requiring fluid balance monitoring during prolonged chamber treatment
- Urinary retention from any cause where the patient cannot void
- Critically ill patient where accurate urine output measurement is needed

Do NOT accept "routine monitoring" without a clinical indication.

### Q48. [4 marks] - Cold Water Immersion

Award 1 mark per correct priority (any 4):
- Horizontal lift from the water (avoid vertical extraction which risks circum-rescue collapse)
- Primary survey: DR ABCDE approach
- Administer high-flow oxygen
- Remove wet clothing carefully; protect from further heat loss (blankets, shelter from wind)
- Handle gently (avoid rough handling which can trigger cardiac arrhythmia in hypothermic patients)
- Active rewarming if available (warm IV fluids, forced warm air blankets)
- Monitor for delayed deterioration (submersion injury may present hours later)
- Prepare for cardiac arrest (hypothermic patients may have very slow/absent pulse; extend pulse check to 60 seconds)

The horizontal lift is a key safety point. Award credit if mentioned.
