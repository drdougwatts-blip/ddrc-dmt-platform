## Section 1: Multiple Choice Questions (25 marks)

*Choose the single best answer for each question.*

**1.** DCI is an umbrella term that covers both decompression sickness (DCS) and arterial gas embolism (AGE).

A) True
B) False
C) Only when the mechanism is unclear
D) Only for treatment purposes

**2.** A diver presents with rapidly worsening bilateral leg weakness 20 minutes after symptoms began during a saturation decompression. The correct DCI classification is:

A) Static musculoskeletal DCI
B) Progressive neurological DCI
C) Spontaneously improving neurological DCI
D) Relapsing cutaneous DCI

**3.** The two "missing" terms in DCI evolution classification (alongside static and spontaneously improving) are:

A) Acute and chronic
B) Relapsing and progressive
C) Mild and severe
D) Type I and Type II

**4.** The maximum recommended oxygen partial pressure for treatment in a recompression chamber is:

A) 1.4 ATA
B) 1.8 ATA
C) 2.4 ATA
D) 2.8 ATA

**5.** In a recompression chamber, airway device cuffs should be filled with saline rather than air because:

A) Saline is sterile
B) Air-filled cuffs change volume with pressure changes
C) Saline provides better lubrication
D) Air-filled cuffs cause tissue damage

**6.** Which of the following is NOT a practical problem when running IV fluids in a hyperbaric chamber?

A) Low ceiling limiting gravitational head
B) Need to remove all air from IV lines
C) Maintaining fluid levels in the drip chamber
D) Increased resistance to fluid flow from chamber pressure

**7.** The earliest symptom of hypercapnia in a diver is typically:

A) Reduced level of consciousness
B) Seizure
C) Headache
D) Chest pain

**8.** Elevated body temperature is a typical feature of DCI.

A) True
B) False

**9.** CNS oxygen toxicity characteristically presents with:

A) Gradual onset cough and breathlessness
B) Seizures, often without warning
C) Headache and nausea over hours
D) Visual disturbance progressing to blindness

**10.** A patient developing chest tightness and dyspnoea during decompression from treatment depth should prompt the DMT to:

A) Continue decompression at a slower rate
B) Wait to see if symptoms resolve spontaneously
C) Inform the chamber operator to halt decompression and hold depth immediately
D) Increase the oxygen flow rate and continue

**11.** A patient who is responsive to pain (P on AVPU) and breathing spontaneously will best tolerate which airway adjunct?

A) Oropharyngeal airway
B) i-gel supraglottic airway
C) Nasopharyngeal airway
D) Endotracheal tube

**12.** The key advantages of a supraglottic airway (e.g. i-gel) over an endotracheal tube for a DMT include:

A) Easier insertion and reduced risk of trauma
B) Better protection against aspiration
C) Superior ventilation in all circumstances
D) All of the above

**13.** When using a suction device to clear an obstructed airway, you should:

A) Suction everywhere systematically
B) Apply suction as you insert the catheter
C) Only suction where you can see the tip or feel where it rests
D) Use the highest suction setting available

**14.** After correctly fitting a cervical collar, manual in-line stabilisation of the head:

A) Can be released
B) Must continue until full spinal immobilisation is achieved
C) Is only needed if the patient is moving
D) Should be replaced by sandbags immediately

**15.** The correct compression depth for adult CPR is:

A) At least 4 cm
B) At least 5 cm
C) At least 6 cm
D) At least 7 cm

**16.** A drop in blood pressure in a bleeding patient is:

A) An early warning sign
B) A late sign indicating significant volume loss
C) Unreliable and should be ignored
D) Only significant if systolic falls below 80 mmHg

**17.** Neurogenic shock differs from hypovolaemic shock in that the patient typically has:

A) Tachycardia and cold, clammy skin
B) Bradycardia and warm, dry skin
C) Tachycardia and warm, flushed skin
D) Normal vital signs

**18.** The correct route for adrenaline in anaphylaxis for a DMT is:

A) Intravenous
B) Intramuscular into the anterolateral thigh
C) Subcutaneous into the upper arm
D) Intramuscular into the deltoid

**19.** A patient suffering from heat stroke requires:

A) Slow cooling over several hours
B) Rapid cooling by any available means
C) Cooling only after IV access is established
D) Oral rehydration before any cooling measures

**20.** When using haemostatic agents, they are contraindicated for use on:

A) Deep limb wounds
B) Eyes, open chest wounds, and open head wounds
C) Any wound with arterial bleeding
D) Contaminated wounds

**21.** Entonox is contraindicated in a patient with a suspected pneumothorax because:

A) It may cause respiratory depression
B) Nitrous oxide diffuses into gas-filled spaces and would enlarge the pneumothorax
C) The oxygen component could cause toxicity
D) It interferes with chest drain function

**22.** The DMAC 01 aide-memoire is used for:

A) Recording dive profiles only
B) Recording and transmitting medical data to shore
C) Logging chamber maintenance
D) Tracking drug stock levels

**23.** When communicating with a physician about a diving casualty, the most effective approach is:

A) Describe only the symptoms and let the physician ask questions
B) Provide a structured handover including situation, background, assessment, and what you need
C) Read out all vital signs first, then describe the history
D) Wait for the physician to direct the conversation

## Section 2: Short Answer Questions (15 marks)

*Answer all questions.*

**26. [5 marks]**

You are the DMT in a saturation system. A diver who surfaced from a bell run 2 hours ago reports worsening tingling in both feet and difficulty standing. List five actions you would take in managing this casualty.

**27. [3 marks]**

Describe three changes or developments in diving medicine practice that have occurred since your last DMT certification that are relevant to your role. (Answers should reflect content covered in the online refresher modules.)

**28. [2 marks]**

What are the two terms that complete the DCI evolution classification? Static, \_\_\_\_\_\_\_, Spontaneously improving, \_\_\_\_\_\_\_

**29. [3 marks]**

You are passing information to a shore-based physician about a crew member with burns. The patient has burns to the whole of the anterior chest and the front and back of both arms. Using the Rule of Nines, calculate the percentage body surface area affected. Show your working.

**30. [2 marks]**

A patient in the chamber develops signs consistent with a tension pneumothorax. List the three most important clinical signs you would report to the physician.
