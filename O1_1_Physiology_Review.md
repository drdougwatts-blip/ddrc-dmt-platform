Musculoskeletal System
Bones, joints, muscles, and the spine

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
Bones and Fracture Relevance

Major bone groups
Fracture types
Long bones: femur, tibia, humerus, radius/ulna
Flat bones: ribs, pelvis, skull
Irregular: vertebrae, facial bones
Why location matters: blood loss varies by site
Closed vs open (compound)
Complicated: associated nerve or vessel injury
Pathological: underlying bone disease
Signs: deformity, swelling, crepitus, loss of function

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
Joints, Muscles, and Spine

Joints and soft tissue
The spinal column
Synovial joints: shoulder, knee, ankle, wrist
Sprain vs dislocation: mechanism and signs
Muscle trauma: strains, compartment syndrome
Crush injury: why muscle breakdown matters (rhabdomyolysis)
Vertebral column: cervical, thoracic, lumbar, sacral
Spinal cord runs within the vertebral canal
C-spine injury: why immobilisation is the priority
Cord damage = permanent deficit below the level of injury

02
Nervous System
Central and peripheral: what the DMT needs to know

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
Central and Peripheral Nervous System
Brain: consciousness, motor control, sensation, speech, pupils
Spinal cord: carries signals between brain and body
Peripheral nerves: motor (movement) and sensory (feeling)
Autonomic nervous system: controls heart rate, blood pressure, gut function
Relevance to shock: sympathetic response drives tachycardia and vasoconstriction

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
Neurological Examination: Why It Matters

What you are testing
Dermatomes simplified
Consciousness level: GCS or AVPU
Motor power: can the patient move against resistance?
Sensation: can they feel light touch?
Reflexes: are normal responses present?
Pupils: size, equality, reactivity to light
Upper limb levels: C5-T1
Trunk levels: roughly follow rib lines
Lower limb levels: L2-S1
You do not need to memorise a full map
Goal: detect change from one exam to the next

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->

The neurological examination detects DCI.
A normal baseline makes changes obvious.
You will practise the full neuro exam on each other during the in-person week. Today is the 'why' behind each test.

03
Cardiovascular System
Heart, vessels, circulation, and blood

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
Heart and Circulation
Four chambers: right atrium/ventricle (lungs), left atrium/ventricle (body)
Coronary arteries: supply the heart muscle itself
Systemic circuit: left ventricle to body and back to right atrium
Pulmonary circuit: right ventricle to lungs and back to left atrium
Cardiac output = heart rate x stroke volume

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
Blood Vessels and Blood

Vessels
Blood
Arteries: high pressure, pulsatile, bright red bleeding
Veins: lower pressure, steady flow, darker blood
Capillaries: gas exchange at tissue level
Major pressure points: femoral, brachial, radial
Red cells: oxygen transport (haemoglobin)
White cells: infection response
Platelets: clotting cascade initiation
Plasma: fluid component, carries proteins
Total volume: approximately 5 litres in an adult

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->

You will control bleeding from arteries, start IV lines in veins, and do chest compressions to maintain circulation.
Every practical skill in the in-person week connects back to this system.

04
Respiratory System
Airway anatomy and gas exchange

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 16 -->
Airway and Lungs
Upper airway: nose, mouth, pharynx, larynx
Lower airway: trachea, bronchi, bronchioles
Alveoli: gas exchange surface (oxygen in, CO2 out)
Diaphragm and intercostals: mechanics of breathing
The tongue is the commonest cause of airway obstruction in an unconscious patient
Relevant skills: OPA, NPA, i-gel, BVM, oxygen delivery

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 17 -->
Gas Exchange and Clinical Links

Normal gas exchange
Clinical relevance
O2 crosses from alveolus to blood
CO2 crosses from blood to alveolus
Driven by partial pressure gradients
Disrupted by: fluid in alveoli, collapse, obstruction
Pneumothorax: air in pleural space, lung collapse
Pulmonary barotrauma: lung overexpansion on ascent
Pulmonary oedema: fluid flooding the alveoli
Oxygen therapy: increasing the driving pressure

05
Ears, Sinuses, and Vestibular Organs
Anatomy relevant to barotrauma and saturation hygiene

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 19 -->
Ear Anatomy and Function

Outer and middle ear
Inner ear and vestibular
External canal to tympanic membrane (eardrum)
Middle ear: ossicles transmit sound vibration
Eustachian tube: pressure equalisation pathway
Dysfunction = squeeze, barotrauma, pain
Cochlea: hearing
Semicircular canals: balance and spatial orientation
Round and oval windows: vulnerable to barotrauma
Inner ear injury = sensorineural hearing loss, vertigo
Alternobaric vertigo: asymmetric equalisation

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 20 -->
Sinuses and Saturation Ear Hygiene
Paranasal sinuses: frontal, maxillary, ethmoid, sphenoid
Air-filled cavities subject to squeeze on descent
Sinus barotrauma: pain, epistaxis, referred toothache
Saturation ear hygiene: humidity, warmth, and earphones create infection risk
Prevention: drying protocols, prophylactic drops, regular checks
Ear problems are the most common medical complaint in saturation
