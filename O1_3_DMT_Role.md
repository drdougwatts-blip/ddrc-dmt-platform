### Notes:

Independent Actions vs Physician Guidance

DMT acts independently
Requires physician guidance
Basic first aid: wound care, splinting, dressings
Initial assessment: ABCDE primary survey
Vital signs monitoring: pulse, BP, SpO2, temp, BM
BLS and AED use in cardiac arrest
Scene safety and casualty protection
Drug administration (all parenteral routes)
IV/IO cannulation and fluid therapy
Clinical decision-making beyond first aid
Diagnosis and treatment planning
Decisions about evacuation or return to diving

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
When to Call the Physician
The answer is always: early
Any condition beyond basic first aid
Any diving-related medical complaint (even if apparently minor)
Any change in a patient's condition
Any uncertainty about what you are dealing with
Never wait until the situation deteriorates before making contact
The physician would rather hear about a minor problem early than a major one late

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
Surface Operations vs Saturation

Surface diving
Saturation diving
DMT on deck or in dive control
Patient accessible for examination
Evacuation to vessel medical facility relatively straightforward
Helicopter medevac an option if needed
DMT may lock into the chamber complex
Working in confined space under pressure
Patient cannot be easily removed from the system
Physician contact via comms or telemedicine
Extended patient care may be required in the chamber

Legal and Regulatory Framework
Brief overview of what governs your practice

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
Regulations and Responsibilities
IMCA D014: International Code of Practice for Offshore Diving
National regulations vary: UK (Diving at Work Regs 1997), Norway, Brazil, etc.
Employer's responsibility: provide trained DMTs, proper equipment, physician access
DMT's responsibility: operate within training, maintain certification, call for help when needed
First Aid at Work: IMCA DMT certificate fulfils UK FAW requirement for 3 years
Insurance and liability: your employer should carry appropriate cover

Scenario Discussion
Three scenarios to explore the boundaries of scope of practice:
Discussion Points

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->

Your duty is to the patient, not to the supervisor's convenience.
If you believe the physician needs to know, make the call. Document that you did. This is not optional.

Key Takeaways
The DMT scope is specifically defined: first aid independently, advanced skills under physician guidance
Call early, not late. The physician would rather be informed than surprised.
Know your key references: DMAC 01, DMAC 15, IMCA D014
Surface and saturation operations create different working conditions but the same principles apply
