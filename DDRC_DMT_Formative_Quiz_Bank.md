# DDRC DMT Course: Formative Quiz Question Bank

**For candidate self-assessment after each online day**

*Not formally assessed. Not counted in contact hours.*

These quizzes are designed for candidates to check their own understanding after each online teaching day. They are not graded and do not contribute to the formal assessment. Results should be used by the candidate to identify areas for further study before the in-person phase.

Each quiz has 12-13 questions in MCQ format. An answer key with brief rationale is provided at the end of each set.

---

## Quiz 1: Foundations

*Complete after Online Day 1 (Physiology, Physics, DMT Role, First Aid Principles)*

**1.** 1 bar is approximately equal to:

A) 750 mmHg
B) 14.5 psi
C) 1000 millibars
D) All of the above

**2.** Henry's law relates to:

A) The pressure-volume relationship of gases
B) Partial pressures of gas mixtures
C) The solubility of gases in liquids
D) The relationship between pressure, volume, and temperature

**3.** 400 ppm is equal to:

A) 40%
B) 4%
C) 0.4%
D) 0.04%

**4.** The correct order for a primary survey is:

A) Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure
B) Breathing, Airway, Circulation, Disability, Exposure
C) Circulation, Airway, Breathing, Disability, Exposure
D) Danger, Response, Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure

**5.** The tidal volume of a healthy adult at rest is approximately:

A) 150 ml
B) 350 ml
C) 500 ml
D) 800 ml

**6.** A normal resting respiratory rate for an adult is:

A) 4 to 12 breaths per minute
B) 12 to 20 breaths per minute
C) 20 to 28 breaths per minute
D) 28 to 36 breaths per minute

**7.** The fibrous sac surrounding the heart is called the:

A) Endocardium
B) Myocardium
C) Pericardium
D) Aorta

**8.** Systolic blood pressure refers to the pressure:

A) In the arteries when the left ventricle contracts
B) In the veins during diastole
C) In the arteries when the heart is relaxed
D) In the pulmonary circulation only

**9.** Tachycardia is generally defined as a heart rate above:

A) 80 bpm
B) 90 bpm
C) 100 bpm
D) 120 bpm

**10.** DMTs are able to use their advanced first aid skills:

A) Independently at any time
B) Only under the guidance of a qualified physician
C) Only when the diving supervisor authorises it
D) Only in a recompression chamber

**11.** The most common cause of upper airway obstruction in an unconscious patient is:

A) Foreign bodies
B) Stomach contents
C) The tongue
D) A swollen epiglottis

**12.** AMPLE is a mnemonic used for:

A) Assessing level of consciousness
B) Gathering a focused patient history
C) Prioritising multiple casualties
D) Documenting vital signs

### Quiz 1 Answer Key

| Q | Ans | Rationale |
|---|-----|-----------|
| 1 | D | All three values are approximate equivalents of 1 bar. |
| 2 | C | Henry's law: the amount of gas dissolved in a liquid is proportional to its partial pressure. |
| 3 | D | 400 parts per million = 400/1,000,000 = 0.04%. Important for gas contamination monitoring. |
| 4 | D | DR ABCDE is the complete primary survey sequence. Scene safety (Danger, Response) comes first. |
| 5 | C | Normal tidal volume is approximately 500 ml per breath. |
| 6 | B | 12-20 breaths per minute is the accepted normal range for adults. |
| 7 | C | The pericardium is the outer protective sac. Relevant to understanding pericardial tamponade. |
| 8 | A | Systolic = peak pressure during ventricular contraction. |
| 9 | C | 100 bpm is the standard threshold for adult tachycardia. |
| 10 | B | D020 Section 2 is explicit: DMTs cannot use advanced skills without physician guidance. |
| 11 | C | The tongue falling back is the most common cause. Basic airway manoeuvres address this first. |
| 12 | B | Allergies, Medications, Past history, Last oral intake, Events. Used during secondary survey. |

## Quiz 2: Diving Medicine

*Complete after Online Day 2 (Gas Toxicity, Barotrauma, DCI)*

**1.** DCI is an umbrella term covering:

A) Decompression sickness only
B) Arterial gas embolism only
C) Both decompression sickness and arterial gas embolism
D) Nitrogen narcosis and oxygen toxicity

**2.** A pneumothorax is a collection of _____ between the pleural linings:

A) Blood
B) Air
C) Fluid
D) Surfactant

**3.** During a rapid uncontrolled ascent, expanding gas in the alveoli causes rupture of the pleural linings. The immediate result is:

A) Arterial gas embolism
B) Pneumothorax
C) Tension pneumothorax
D) Subcutaneous emphysema

**4.** The four clinical conditions that can result from pulmonary barotrauma are pneumothorax, subcutaneous emphysema, mediastinal emphysema, and:

A) Haemothorax
B) Pleural effusion
C) Arterial gas embolism
D) Pneumomediastinum

**5.** Bubble formation in tissues affects the body by:

A) Blocking arteries and small vessels
B) Tearing tissue
C) Causing blood thickening (sludging)
D) All of the above

**6.** Signs and symptoms of hypercapnia include:

A) Increased breathing rate, nausea, headache
B) Cool clammy skin, slow breathing, cyanosed lips
C) Pallor, irritable cough, tinnitus
D) Cyanosis, slow pulse, chest pain

**7.** The correct site for emergency needle decompression in a tension pneumothorax is:

A) 2nd intercostal space, mid-clavicular line
B) 6th intercostal space, mid-axillary line
C) Anywhere in the upper chest
D) It should never be attempted by a DMT

**8.** Oxygen toxicity affecting the central nervous system (CNS) is most likely to present with:

A) Gradual onset of breathlessness
B) Seizures, often without warning
C) Slow deterioration over hours
D) Persistent cough and chest tightness

**9.** The intra-pleural pressure difference required to cause pulmonary barotrauma is equivalent to approximately:

A) 70 mmHg, 3 fsw, or 93 mb
B) 140 mmHg, 6 fsw, or 186 mb
C) 210 mmHg, 9 fsw, or 279 mb
D) 350 mmHg, 15 fsw, or 465 mb

**10.** Nitrogen narcosis is best described as:

A) A permanent neurological injury from nitrogen exposure
B) A reversible impairment of cognitive function at depth due to the narcotic effect of nitrogen
C) An allergic reaction to high nitrogen partial pressures
D) A form of decompression sickness

**11.** Decompression illness involves many signs and symptoms. Which of the following is NOT a typical feature?

A) Fatigue
B) Joint pain
C) Personality changes
D) Elevated body temperature

**12.** High Pressure Nervous Syndrome (HPNS) is associated with:

A) Shallow air diving
B) Deep helium diving
C) Oxygen rebreather use
D) Surface decompression

### Quiz 2 Answer Key

| Q | Ans | Rationale |
|---|-----|-----------|
| 1 | C | DCI encompasses both DCS and AGE. Important for correct terminology. |
| 2 | B | Air in the pleural space = pneumothorax. Blood = haemothorax. |
| 3 | B | At the instant of rupture, air enters the pleural space. AGE and tension are secondary developments. |
| 4 | C | AGE is the fourth and most dangerous consequence of pulmonary barotrauma. |
| 5 | D | Bubbles cause mechanical, vascular, and haematological effects. |
| 6 | A | Classic CO2 retention presentation. Headache is often the earliest symptom. |
| 7 | A | 2nd ICS MCL is the standard taught site. The 4th/5th ICS anterior axillary line is an alternative. |
| 8 | B | CNS O2 toxicity presents with seizures (Paul Bert effect). Pulmonary O2 toxicity is the gradual form. |
| 9 | A | All three values (~1 msw depth change) are approximately equivalent. Shows how little overpressure is needed. |
| 10 | B | Narcosis is entirely reversible on ascent. Not an injury. Not DCS. |
| 11 | D | Fever is not a typical DCI feature. The others are all recognised presentations. |
| 12 | B | HPNS occurs at depth during helium diving, typically below 150 msw. |

## Quiz 3: Medical Emergencies and Pharmacology

*Complete after Online Day 3 (Shock, Burns, Thermal/Drowning, Drugs, Remaining Topics)*

**1.** Shock is best defined as:

A) Low blood pressure
B) Inadequate tissue perfusion
C) Loss of consciousness
D) Severe blood loss

**2.** The classic signs of hypovolaemic shock include:

A) Flushed skin, bradycardia, capillary refill under 2 seconds
B) Alert, slow respirations, warm skin
C) Cool and clammy skin, tachycardia, capillary refill over 2 seconds
D) BP 136/84, pulse 60-80, respirations 12-20

**3.** Not all types of shock present with pale, cool, clammy skin. Which type typically presents with warm, dry skin?

A) Hypovolaemic
B) Anaphylactic
C) Neurogenic
D) Cardiogenic

**4.** A burn involving the epidermis and dermis, producing blisters and pain, is classified as:

A) Superficial
B) Partial thickness
C) Deep tissue
D) Full thickness

**5.** If treating a chemical burn from an unknown substance, you should:

A) Identify the substance before treating
B) Apply a neutralising agent
C) Irrigate vigorously with cool water for at least 20 minutes
D) Cover the area and transport immediately

**6.** Chest pain during physical exertion that is relieved by rest is most consistent with:

A) Myocardial infarction
B) Angina pectoris
C) Pulmonary embolism
D) Tension pneumothorax

**7.** When delivering oxygen using a non-rebreather mask, the constant flow rate should be:

A) 5 litres per minute
B) 8 litres per minute
C) 12 litres per minute
D) 15 litres per minute

**8.** Entonox (50% N2O / 50% O2) is contraindicated in:

A) Simple fractures
B) Renal colic
C) Pneumothorax
D) Labour pain

**9.** The correct route for adrenaline in anaphylaxis is:

A) Intravenous
B) Intramuscular (anterolateral thigh)
C) Subcutaneous
D) Intramuscular (deltoid)

**10.** A patient with a suspected spinal cord injury at the thoracic level develops urinary retention. The most appropriate intervention for a DMT is:

A) Encourage the patient to try harder
B) Restrict oral fluids
C) Insert a urinary catheter as directed by the physician
D) Apply suprapubic pressure

**11.** When preparing an intramuscular injection, the DMT should:

A) Use the largest available needle
B) Draw up the drug, then change to a clean needle for injection
C) Inject into the nearest available muscle
D) Aspirate briefly before injecting to check for blood vessel placement

**12.** Hypothermia in a diver recovered from cold water should be managed by:

A) Rapid rewarming in a hot bath
B) Gentle handling, removal of wet clothing, gradual active rewarming
C) Vigorous rubbing of the limbs to restore circulation
D) Immediate CPR regardless of cardiac rhythm

**13.** COVID and other critical viral illnesses are relevant to the DMT because:

A) Saturation systems create close-contact environments where transmission risk is high
B) PPE requirements change the way practical skills are performed
C) Both infected and close-contact personnel may need to be managed in isolation
D) All of the above

### Quiz 3 Answer Key

| Q | Ans | Rationale |
|---|-----|-----------|
| 1 | B | Shock = inadequate perfusion. BP is a late indicator. Blood loss is one cause, not the definition. |
| 2 | C | Cool/clammy/tachy/slow cap refill = sympathetic compensation for volume loss. |
| 3 | C | Neurogenic shock causes loss of sympathetic tone, leading to vasodilation (warm, dry skin) and bradycardia. |
| 4 | B | Partial thickness = epidermis + dermis. Blisters and pain present because nerve endings are intact but damaged. |
| 5 | C | Never use neutralising agents. Copious water irrigation is always correct regardless of the chemical. |
| 6 | B | Angina = exertional pain relieved by rest. MI pain is not relieved by rest. |
| 7 | D | 15 lpm is the standard flow rate for a non-rebreather mask. |
| 8 | C | N2O diffuses into gas-filled spaces. In pneumothorax, this would expand the air collection. |
| 9 | B | IM into the anterolateral thigh is the recommended route. IV adrenaline is a physician-level decision. |
| 10 | C | Catheterisation is indicated but only under physician direction. Tests scope of practice awareness. |
| 11 | D | Aspiration helps confirm the needle is not in a blood vessel. Standard IM injection technique. |
| 12 | B | Rough handling risks arrhythmia in hypothermic patients. Gentle handling and controlled rewarming are essential. |
| 13 | D | All three factors are operationally relevant. D020 includes COVID and viral illness management explicitly. |
