## Welcome

Thank you for enrolling on the DDRC Diver Medic Technician (DMT) course. This qualification is approved by IMCA under document D020 and is recognised internationally for offshore and commercial diving operations.

This pack gives you a brief overview of how the course works, what you need to have ready, and a short self-assessment quiz to help you identify any areas where you might want to do some background reading before we start.

### How the Course Works

The course is a 60-hour hybrid programme delivered in two phases:

| | Phase 1: Online Theory | Phase 2: In-Person Practical |
|---|---|---|
| **Duration** | 22.5 hours over 4 consecutive days (Monday to Thursday) | 37.5 hours over 5 consecutive days (Monday to Friday) |
| **Format** | Live video sessions with your instructor. Camera on throughout. Interactive, not recorded lectures. | Hands-on practical skills at DDRC Plymouth. Manikins, clinical equipment, chamber familiarisation. |
| **Location** | You will attend from your own location. | You will attend in person at DDRC Plymouth. |

Your total commitment is 9 days: 4 online followed by 5 in person. The gap between finishing the online phase and starting the in-person phase should be no more than 8 weeks.

### Online Week at a Glance

| Day | Content | Hours | Instructor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | Physiology, Diving Physics, DMT Role, First Aid Principles | 7.5 | SI |
| **Tuesday** | Gas Toxicity, Barotrauma and Ears, Medical Topics | 6.5 | SI |
| **Wednesday** | Shock and Burns, Thermal/Drowning/Environmental (AM only) | 3.5 | SI |
| **Thursday** | Decompression Illness (DMP), Pharmacology (DMP) | 5.0 | DMP |

Monday to Wednesday are led by a Senior DMT Instructor (SI). Thursday is led by a Diving Medical Physician (DMP) and is a shared session with any refresher candidates running that week. Wednesday is a half day *for you*; you finish after the morning sessions.

### In-Person Week at a Glance

Five full days at DDRC Plymouth, 0830 to 1715 each day. The week builds from basic skills (airway management, vital signs) through to complex scenarios. Day 5 includes your formal assessment: a written examination in the morning and a practical scenario in the afternoon.

You will practise on manikins and simulation equipment. All competencies are signed off by your instructor as you progress through the week. There is no single high-stakes practical test at the end; your skills are assessed continuously.

## What to Expect

### Session Format

Online sessions are live and interactive. Your instructor will use slides, diagrams, and case discussions. You will be asked questions, put into breakout discussions, and expected to contribute. This is not a course you can passively watch.

Most candidates are experienced divers, diving supervisors, or life support technicians. The instructors know this and will build on your existing knowledge. You do not need a medical background, but the pace will be brisk because there is a lot of ground to cover.

### Assessment

Your formal assessment takes place on the final day of the in-person phase (Day 5). It consists of a written examination (1 hour, multiple choice and short answer, 70% pass mark) and one practical scenario observed by an independent examiner. Throughout the in-person week, your instructor will also sign off individual competencies as you demonstrate them during practical sessions.

There is no formal assessment during the online phase. Formative quizzes are available after each online day so you can check your own understanding, but these do not count towards your result.

### The Thursday DMP Session

Thursday is taught by a Diving Medical Physician and covers decompression illness and pharmacology. This is a shared session: refresher candidates will also be present. The teaching is delivered at full depth and covers both foundational concepts and current developments. Do not be concerned about the mixed group; the content is the same for everyone.

## Self-Assessment: Answers

| Q | Ans | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **C** | Scene safety always comes first. You cannot help anyone if you become a casualty yourself. |
| 2 | **B** | 60-100 bpm is the accepted normal resting range for adults. |
| 3 | **B** | Boyle's law: at constant temperature, pressure and volume are inversely proportional. Fundamental to understanding squeeze and barotrauma. |
| 4 | **C** | The trachea divides into the left and right main bronchi at the carina. |
| 5 | **C** | Disability refers to a brief neurological assessment: level of consciousness, pupils, limb movement. |
| 6 | **B** | Oxygenated arterial blood is bright red. Deoxygenated venous blood is darker. |
| 7 | **B** | The recovery position keeps the airway open and allows fluids to drain, reducing aspiration risk. |
| 8 | **B** | Normal adult systolic BP is typically 100-140 mmHg. You will learn to measure this in the practical phase. |
| 9 | **C** | Henry's law explains how gas dissolves into tissues under pressure, which is the basis of decompression theory. |
| 10 | **C** | Anaphylaxis is a medical emergency requiring immediate treatment. You will cover this in detail during the course. |
| 11 | **B** | 12-20 breaths per minute. Faster than this in a resting adult is abnormal and needs investigation. |
| 12 | **C** | Tachycardia = fast heart rate (generally above 100 bpm). Bradycardia = slow heart rate. |

**If you scored 8 or more:** You have a solid baseline. The course will build on this.

**If you scored 5 to 7:** You will be fine, but a quick review of basic first aid and gas laws before Monday would help.

**If you scored below 5:** Consider looking at a basic first aid manual (St John Ambulance or Red Cross) and the introductory chapters of any recreational diving physics textbook. The course starts from the beginning, but a head start will make the first day easier.
