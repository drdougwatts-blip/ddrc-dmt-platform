01
Telemedicine
Application offshore and inside a complex

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
Telemedicine Systems
Video consultation: real-time link between DMT and shore-based physician
Store-and-forward: send images, ECGs, or data for later review
Real-time vital sign transmission: automated data feed to physician
Offshore challenges: bandwidth, satellite delay, connectivity gaps
Inside a hyperbaric complex: camera placement, comms integration, image quality
Telemedicine supports but does not replace the voice link between DMT and physician
Practical telemedicine use is assessed in-person

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
Using Telemedicine Effectively

Before the consultation
During the consultation
Prepare: vital signs, history, examination findings documented
Check equipment: camera, lighting, connection quality
DMAC 01 completed and ready to read to the physician
Anticipate what the physician will want to see
Show, do not just tell: point the camera at the patient, wound, equipment
Structured handover: use SBAR or ATMIST format
Confirm instructions by reading them back
Document the physician's decisions and the time of contact

Diagnostic Technology

Point-of-care testing
Monitoring equipment

03
ECG and Cardiac Monitoring

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
ECG Lead Placement
DMTs place ECG leads. DMTs do not interpret the ECG.
The physician reads and interprets the tracing remotely via telemedicine
12-lead ECG: full assessment, used when equipment and time allow
3-lead ECG: continuous monitoring during treatment or observation
Correct lead placement is essential for a usable tracing
Practical ECG lead placement is taught in-person (Day 3 or Day 4)
Transmitting ECG data: photograph, digital transmission, or telemedicine link

### Notes:

Wound Management and Airway Advances

Wound management
Airway developments
Modern haemostatic agents: celox, quikclot
Chest seal devices: vented chest seals for open pneumothorax
Tourniquet developments: CAT, SOFTT-W
Wound closure advances: tissue adhesive, steri-strips
i-gel: standard supraglottic airway for non-intubation trained rescuers
Video laryngoscopy: if available (not standard DMT equipment)
New items added to DMAC 15 kit: check current publication
Practical airway skills are taught in-person Day 1

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
Hyperbaric Environment: Equipment Safety
Not all equipment is safe under pressure or in oxygen-enriched atmospheres
Electronic devices: sparking risk in high oxygen environments
Battery-powered devices: check manufacturer guidance for hyperbaric use
Monitoring equipment certified for hyperbaric use: ensure you know what is approved
Practical workarounds: if standard equipment cannot go in the chamber, what alternatives exist?
Incident examples: equipment failures, fires, near-misses in hyperbaric environments

Key Takeaways
Telemedicine: prepare, show the physician what they need to see, confirm instructions
Point-of-care testing: blood glucose, viral tests, urinalysis. Know how to run and interpret them.
ECG: place the leads correctly, transmit the data, let the physician interpret
Hyperbaric safety: not all equipment is chamber-safe. Know what is approved.
Hands-on telemedicine, ECG, and testing practice happens in-person
